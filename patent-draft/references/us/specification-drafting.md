# U.S. utility specification drafting

## Disclosure baseline

Draft the description so a person skilled in the relevant art can recognize that
the inventor possessed the claimed subject matter and can make and use its full
scope without undue experimentation. Written description and enablement are
separate requirements. A desired claim breadth does not supply missing disclosure.
`[LAW US-35-112(a)] [USPTO-PRACTICE US-MPEP-2163, US-MPEP-2164]`

For every independent-claim limitation, identify in the support map:

- the exact supplied source and location;
- the corresponding structure, material, acts, data, or relationship;
- how the embodiment performs the recited function;
- any supported variants and the limits of the disclosed genus; and
- any parameter, range, test method, or operating condition needed to reproduce
  the result.

Do not convert a single species into a genus, a result into a mechanism, or a
prototype observation into a universal property without supplied support.
`[DRAFTING]`

The statute still requires disclosure of the best mode contemplated by the
inventor. Ask for and disclose it when applicable; do not suppress it as a style
choice. Do not invent a preferred embodiment when the evidence does not identify
one. `[LAW US-35-112(a)]`

## Description structure

Use the section order applicable to the case under 37 CFR 1.77 and MPEP § 608.01.
For a typical substantive draft, prepare:

1. title;
2. cross-reference and government-support statements only from confirmed facts;
3. background, limited to supported context and problems;
4. summary consistent with the planned claims;
5. brief description of every supplied or planned drawing;
6. detailed description of supported embodiments and operation;
7. claims on a separate working section; and
8. abstract on a separate working section.

Do not fabricate priority claims, government funding, joint-research facts,
incorporation-by-reference statements, or prior-art admissions. Route missing
administrative facts to unresolved decisions. `[RULE US-37-P1 §§1.71–1.77]
[USPTO-PRACTICE US-MPEP-608]`

## Drafting the technical teaching

- Define important terms expressly when the supplied evidence supports a special
  meaning; otherwise use terminology consistently with its technical context.
- Describe relationships and operation, not a parts inventory alone. Connect each
  claimed feature to the embodiment that realizes it.
- State alternatives as alternatives, and only when each is supported. Preserve
  combinations that were actually disclosed; do not create arbitrary mix-and-
  match permutations.
- Give reproducible ranges, units, tolerances, measurement methods, and boundary
  rules where they affect operation. Do not manufacture numerical endpoints.
- Include at least one supported operative embodiment or example whose detail is
  commensurate with the planned claim scope. A working example is not a license to
  generalize beyond the teaching.
- Use embodiment-level wording when a statement is not true across the full
  disclosure. Avoid unsupported assertions that every embodiment is required to
  achieve a particular advantage.

`[LAW US-35-112(a)] [RULE US-37-P1 §1.71] [USPTO-PRACTICE
US-MPEP-608.01(p), US-MPEP-2164.08] [DRAFTING]`

## Drawings and reference numerals

Prepare a drawings brief whenever drawings are needed to understand the invention.
The drawing plan must show each claimed feature, while conventional detail may be
represented symbolically when enough for understanding. Keep figure numbers,
reference numerals, terminology, and the detailed description synchronized.
`[LAW US-35-113] [RULE US-37-P1 §§1.81–1.84] [USPTO-PRACTICE
US-MPEP-608.02(d)]`

The drafting agent prepares the content brief and cross-check; it does not certify
formal drawing-sheet compliance. Route production drawing formalities to
`patent-audit`. `[HOUSE-STYLE]`

## Abstract

Draft a single concise paragraph describing the technical disclosure, not legal
scope. It should normally identify the technical subject, central organization or
steps, and resulting technical operation. Do not include alleged merits,
speculative uses, comparisons with prior art, or claim-style language. Keep it at
150 words or fewer for the U.S. application draft. `[RULE US-37-P1 §1.72(b)]
[USPTO-PRACTICE US-MPEP-608.01(b)]`

The abstract cannot cure missing support in the description or claims.
`[DRAFTING]`
